These are the hosted, rented options. They are excellent, and they are not the only ones — which is the point of the next section.

Owning the stack

Optional, and worth an evening of your life. Nothing below requires a budget, a server, or a computer science degree — a recent laptop or tablet is already enough hardware to run a real model without sending a single word to anyone else.

  • Hugging Face — the open commons for models and datasets. Browse it once and the phrase "an AI" stops meaning one company's product.
  • Ollama or LM Studio — the shortest path from "models are mysterious" to a capable one answering you offline, on your own machine.
  • GitHub plus Cloudflare Pages or GitHub Pages — free ways to put what you make on the open web under a name you control. This very site is built that way, and its source is public.
  • Your own hardware. The neural accelerators shipping in current phones, tablets, and laptops are the reason local AI is about to be ordinary. Check what yours can already do before renting the same capability by the month.

The mini-lesson ends with a live terminal wired to a small open model running on private hardware — a worked example of exactly this, and a reminder of how readily small models make things up.

Practical notes

Every tool used in this course has a free tier; nobody needs to pay for anything to take it. Set accounts up with an email address you are comfortable using. Never paste private information — yours or anyone else's — into a hosted AI tool; we cover why in Module 6, and a locally run model is one honest answer to that problem.
