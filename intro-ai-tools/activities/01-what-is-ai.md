# Activity ideas: Module 1 — What is AI? Past, Present, and Future

Part of the [activity idea library](../ACTIVITY_CONCEPTS.md) — brainstorming
material, not finished lesson plans. Scaffold tags, floor/stretch notes, and the
three pillars (agency · ownership · humanization) are defined in the top-level map.

> **Deepening this file:** each module file is independent so a research agent can
> go deep on one lesson at a time. When adding ideas: keep the entry format, verify
> every link is live, mark inventions as *(adapted)*, and prefer activities with a
> low floor, a high ceiling, and a workplace or community tie-in.


- **The Intelligent Piece of Paper** `[show→assist]` — Play tic-tac-toe against a sheet of
  if/then rules, then discuss why it "won" without thinking. Floor: just play. Stretch:
  write the rule sheet yourself. Workplace: rule-based automation (email filters, decision
  trees) vs. real understanding. [ISTE unplugged activities](https://iste.org/blog/3-unplugged-activities-for-teaching-about-ai), [CS Unplugged](https://classic.csunplugged.org/activities/the-turing-test/)
- **Live Turing Test role-play** `[assist]` — Volunteers play scripted "computer," free
  "human," and messenger; class guesses which is which. Floor: vote and discuss. Stretch:
  design interrogation questions that expose the computer. [Harvey Mudd MyCS lesson plan](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/MyCS/MyCS_all_handouts/Unit%201/PDF%20Turing%20Test%20Lesson%20Plan.pdf)
- **"Human or Not?" chat game** `[solo]` — 2-minute anonymous web chats; guess bot or
  human, debrief the tells. Floor: play and guess. Stretch: log which linguistic tells
  worked. Workplace: spotting AI-written correspondence. [humanornot.io](https://humanornot.io/)
- **Bot Buster card game** `[assist]` — Reverse Turing test: players write responses
  trying to sound like AI; judge must spot the one real AI output. Student-created at
  Northeastern's game design course. [Northeastern News](https://news.northeastern.edu/2025/03/04/can-you-identify-ai/)
- **Predict-the-next-word demo** `[show]` — Type a text on the projector, pause
  mid-sentence, class shouts the next word, reveal autocomplete. Sets up next-token
  prediction and why AI drafts need review. Stretch: articulate *why* some words are more
  probable. [Digital Technologies Hub](https://www.digitaltechnologieshub.edu.au/teach-and-assess/classroom-resources/lesson-ideas/exploring-predictive-text/)
- **"The mitochondria is the…" completion exercise** `[assist]` — Community-college-authored
  walkthrough of one-word-at-a-time prediction, ending on "were you surprised AI is just
  predicting, not understanding?" Stretch: extend the chain token by token to watch errors
  compound. [Merced College — Demystifying AI OER](https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Courses/Merced_College/Demystifying_AI:_A_Practical_Introduction_for_Instructors/01:_An_Introduction_to_AI/1.01:_What_is_a_language_model/1.1.01:_How_language_models_generate_text_by_predicting_the_next_word)
- **AI history interactive timeline** `[solo]` — Explore/extend a Turing-to-ChatGPT
  timeline; add a one-line reaction per milestone. Stretch: research and add a missing
  milestone with a source. Workplace: frames the current moment as one of several hype
  cycles. [BookWidgets AI lesson roundup](https://www.bookwidgets.com/blog/2025/08/15-ready-to-use-lesson-plans-to-teach-students-about-artificial-intelligence)
- **Feature-checklist pattern-recognition simulation** `[assist]` — Identify a "mystery"
  cartoon image using only a feature checklist — how classifiers match features without
  seeing. Workplace: why resume screeners make categorical errors. [ISTE](https://iste.org/blog/3-unplugged-activities-for-teaching-about-ai)
- **Teachable Machine: train your own classifier** `[assist→solo]` — No-code image/sound
  classifier trained live; then feed it bad/biased data and watch it fail. Floor: scripted
  two-category build. Stretch: 3+ categories, stress-test edge cases. Workplace:
  garbage-in-garbage-out, training-data bias. [teachablemachine.withgoogle.com](https://teachablemachine.withgoogle.com/), [Day of AI](https://dayofai.org/units/how-do-machines-learn)
- **Quick, Draw!** `[solo→assist]` — Play Google's sketch-guessing neural net, then
  examine the open dataset and discuss failures. [aipedagogy.org assignment](https://aipedagogy.org/assignment/exploring-neural-networks-through-googles-quick-draw/)
- **Spot the AI: real vs. fake image quiz** `[solo→assist]` — News Literacy Project's
  "AI or not?" quiz plus debrief on visual tells and how fast they're disappearing.
  Stretch: predict which tells stop working next year. [newslit.org AI or not?](https://newslit.org/news-and-research/ai-or-not/)
- **K-H-W-L chart** `[show/solo]` — Know / *Heard* / Want-to-know / Learned — the "Heard"
  column separates confirmed knowledge from hype; revisit at session end.
  Self-differentiating. [News Literacy Project lesson](https://newslit.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/AI-or-not_Lesson-Plan.pdf)
- **AI vocabulary flashcards/crossword** `[solo]` — Algorithm, token, hallucination,
  prompt, etc. Stretch: define in own words + a workplace example each. [BookWidgets](https://www.bookwidgets.com/blog/2025/08/15-ready-to-use-lesson-plans-to-teach-students-about-artificial-intelligence)
- **"Can you fool the AI?" hallucination hunt** `[solo]` — Craft prompts designed to
  elicit a false statement or fake citation; present your best fool. Floor: obvious trick
  questions. Stretch: subtle traps + explain why the model failed. [BookWidgets](https://www.bookwidgets.com/blog/2025/08/15-ready-to-use-lesson-plans-to-teach-students-about-artificial-intelligence)
- **Algorithmic bias mini-sequence** `[assist]` — Three 15-min escalating activities:
  bias in search autocomplete → video recommendations → chatbot answers. [Bronx CC AI Faculty Guide](https://bcc-cuny.libguides.com/FacultyAI/literacy)
- **Warming Up to AI: low-stakes first prompts** `[assist→solo]` — Playful first-contact
  prompts (fusion recipe, fantastical vacation, silly poem) to build psychological safety
  before workplace tasks. Explicitly designed by Stanford to reduce first-use anxiety.
  [Stanford Teaching Commons](https://teachingcommons.stanford.edu/teaching-guides/artificial-intelligence-teaching-guide/warming-ai)
- **Elements of AI Ch. 1 as pre-work** `[solo→assist]` — The course-recommended free text;
  self-paced definitions + philosophy reflection, discussed in class. [elementsofai.com](https://www.elementsofai.com/)
- **"Augmentable vs. automatable" job-task discussion** `[assist]` *(adapted)* — Small
  groups map parts of their own jobs/majors to augment vs. automate; directly addresses
  job anxiety. [Harvard GSE framing](https://www.gse.harvard.edu/ideas/news/24/02/ai-wont-take-your-job-if-you-know-about-ia)
- 🌟 **Browsing Hugging Face: what AI tools are made of** `[show→solo]` *(instructor-seeded)* —
  Guided tour of models, datasets, Spaces, and model cards as the "parts bin" of AI tools;
  then a tight scavenger hunt ("find a model that translates speech; read one model card;
  try one Space"). Floor: click one demo. Stretch: compare model sizes, run a Space.
  [huggingface.co](https://huggingface.co/)
- 🌟 **Run a model on your own hardware** `[show→solo]` *(instructor-seeded)* — Install
  LM Studio (or Ollama) on a laptop, download a small open model, and chat with it — no
  account, no cloud, works on an airplane. Compare its answers to a frontier cloud model:
  what does 8B-on-your-laptop get right and wrong? Floor: watch the demo, try the
  instructor's machine. Stretch: install on your own device, compare model sizes and
  quantizations. Workplace/ownership: the "AI = someone else's server" assumption breaks —
  capable local AI is already in current phones and laptops, and understanding that is
  what turns customers into owners. [lmstudio.ai](https://lmstudio.ai/), [ollama.com](https://ollama.com/)
- 🌟 **What is an AI gateway?** `[show]` *(instructor-seeded)* — Trace a question from the
  course site's own ask-widget through the Cloudflare Function to a self-hosted LiteLLM
  gateway to a model — routing, keys, rate limits, model tiers: "this is roughly what
  happens inside ChatGPT." The widget's 429 "recharging" meter is a live teachable moment
  about rate limiting. Workplace: why company AI tools have quotas, policies, and costs.
  (Instructor-driven demo; don't have the whole class hammer the widget at once.)
