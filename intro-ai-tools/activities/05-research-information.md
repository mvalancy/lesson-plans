Part of the [activity idea library](../ACTIVITY_CONCEPTS.md) — brainstorming
material, not finished lesson plans. Scaffold tags, floor/stretch notes, and the
three pillars (agency · ownership · humanization) are defined in the top-level map.

*(Source of the mini-lesson — keep framing consistent with it.)*

- **The Great Citation Investigation** `[assist groups]` — 3 citations, 1 AI-fabricated;
  find the fake via databases/Scholar in ~30 min. Stretch: a subtly altered real citation
  instead of a pure fake. [Georgia Southern LibGuide](https://georgiasouthern.libguides.com/c.php?g=1380906&p=10211384)
- **Humans vs. AI citation showdown** `[assist→solo]` — Groups build citations manually or
  via AI; peer-review to spot which were AI-made; debate who cites better. [Georgia Southern](https://georgiasouthern.libguides.com/c.php?g=1380906&p=10211384)
- **The ZODIAC test** `[show→assist]` — Six-criterion source evaluation (Zooming in, Other
  opinions, Date, Intended audience, Author, Consistency) + AI-content cues. Stretch:
  apply it to a Deep Research output. [Georgia Southern](https://georgiasouthern.libguides.com/c.php?g=1380906&p=10211384)
- **AI tool jigsaw (Elicit / Perplexity / ChatGPT / database)** `[assist]` — Each group
  tests one tool on the same question; class builds a comparison chart. [FIU Libraries lesson plans](https://library.fiu.edu/AI-ACRL/lesson-plans)
- **Human vs. ChatGPT search keywords** `[assist]` — Half generate keywords manually,
  half via AI; test both sets live in a database. [FIU Libraries](https://library.fiu.edu/AI-ACRL/lesson-plans)
- **Scholarship-as-conversation comparison** `[assist]` — Real journal article vs. an
  AI-generated "academic analysis" with 5 citations; flag the questionable ones. Form of
  expertise without substance. [FIU Libraries](https://library.fiu.edu/AI-ACRL/lesson-plans)
- **Chatbot claim triangulation (20 min)** `[assist→solo]` — Extract 3 claims from a
  chatbot answer; verify each across Google, Wikipedia, and a library database; refine
  the prompt from what you learned. [Bronx CC AI workshop](https://bcc-cuny.libguides.com/AIworkshop/activities)
- **Critique the Bot** `[show→assist]` — Rubric-evaluate an instructor-generated AI text
  for accuracy/bias/quality, as if reviewing a colleague's draft. Stretch: improve it via
  re-prompting and compare. [RPI AI Literacy Toolkit](https://guides.lib.rpi.edu/ai-literacy/lesson-plans)
- **Hallucinated references lab** `[assist]` — Prompt for text with citations; verify each
  as Valid/Suspicious/Hallucinated via Scholar and DOI lookups; then re-run with a
  source-constrained prompt and quantify the improvement. [WAC Clearinghouse lesson](https://wacclearinghouse.org/repository/collections/continuing-experiments/august-2025/ai-literacy/understanding-avoiding-hallucinated-references/)
- **Fact-Check It! verification jigsaw** `[assist]` — Expert groups each learn one skill
  (reverse image search, lateral reading, quote tracing), then mixed groups fact-check a
  real viral claim together. [News Literacy Project](https://newslit.org/news-and-research/fact-check-it/)
- **AI or not? real vs. generated media** `[show→solo]` — K-H-W-L chart + quiz + lateral/
  upstream reading extension on 6 social posts. [News Literacy Project lesson plan](https://newslit.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/AI-or-not_Lesson-Plan.pdf)
- **MediaWise: separating fact from fiction** `[show→assist]` — Video lesson + applying
  long-tail keyword search, lateral and upstream reading to sample posts. [PBS NewsHour Classroom](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/classroom/lesson-plans/2023/02/lesson-plan-how-to-separate-fact-from-fiction-in-a-new-era-of-artificial-intelligence)
- **CRAAP race / source bracket** `[assist]` *(adapted)* — Timed competitive source
  evaluation; head-to-head "which source wins on reliability" debates. Stretch: real
  article vs. AI look-alike. [CRAAP guides](https://libguides.nova.edu/c.php?g=1236356&p=9047251)
- **Lateral reading / SIFT practice** `[show→assist]` — Leave the page, open tabs,
  investigate the source — instructor models the tab-switching live. Stretch: SIFT a
  chatbot's cited source. [Civic Online Reasoning (Stanford/DIG)](https://cor.inquirygroup.org/)
- **NotebookLM class-materials Q&A** `[assist]` — Upload readings, generate study guide /
  quiz / Audio Overview; contrast source-grounded AI with open-web chatbots. Floor:
  pre-loaded notebook. Stretch: curate your own source set and test its refusal boundary.
  [Northeastern AI gallery](https://learning.northeastern.edu/ai-gallery-post-beyond-lectures-and-the-textbook)
- **NotebookLM "train the bot" podcast** `[solo teams]` *(adapted)* — Curate credible
  sources → notebook → Audio Overview that teaches the class; bad source set = bad
  podcast, so the real lesson is curation. Stretch: justify rejecting one unreliable
  source.
- **Search engine vs. answer engine bake-off** `[assist→solo]` *(adapted)* — Same
  question through Google, Perplexity, and a chatbot; compare steps, citations, and
  handling of a recent-news question.
- **Deep Research report critique** `[solo→assist]` *(adapted)* — Run a Deep Research
  tool, then treat its report like a student paper: verify 3 claims against originals,
  flag the unsupported, rate trustworthiness. Mirrors reviewing AI market research before
  a business decision.
- **Privacy policy comparison** `[solo groups]` — Compare AI platforms' data-collection
  policies; defend which gives users most control, citing clause language. Workplace:
  what happens to confidential data pasted into public tools. [Georgia Southern](https://georgiasouthern.libguides.com/c.php?g=1380906&p=10211384)
