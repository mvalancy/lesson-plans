# Activity ideas: Module 3 — Working with AI Images, Audio, and Video

Part of the [activity idea library](../ACTIVITY_CONCEPTS.md). The lab should feel like
*making and doubting*: everyone creates something real for their community, and everyone
gets fooled at least once by synthetic media — on purpose, in a safe room.

## Start here — the picks

### 1. Draw a scientist, then generate one `[assist]` · 25 min

The module's bias moment, run on the students' own data. Nothing said in a lecture about
"training data bias" lands like a tally sheet the class produced itself.

**Run it:**
1. Everyone sketches "a scientist" on paper — 60 seconds, stick figures welcome (3 min).
2. Generate "a scientist" several times in any free image tool; tally the demographics
   of both the drawings and the generations on the whiteboard (10 min).
3. Compare both tallies to real labor statistics. Where did the model's defaults come
   from? Where did *ours*? (10 min)

**Floor:** compare one drawing and one generation.
**Stretch:** cite the documented occupation-bias studies; design counter-bias prompts
and re-test.
**They walk away with:** bias as something measured, not asserted.
Sources: [twinpics.ai classroom methods](https://twinpics.ai/blog/teach-ai-literacy-classroom-methods), [Art Education study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00043125.2024.2330340)

### 2. Community flyer / logo sprint `[solo]` · 35 min

Empowerment made concrete: in one sitting, produce a design artifact a real small
business or community group could use.

**Run it:**
1. One-sentence brief, invented or real: a taquería's grand opening, a little league
   fundraiser, a neighbor's dog-walking service (2 min).
2. Twenty-minute sprint in any AI design tool — Canva's template flow as the floor,
   Figma or a raw image model for the stretch tier (20 min).
3. Gallery critique: legibility, brand fit, and the honest question — "would you pay for
   this?" (15 min)

**Floor:** template-guided flow.
**Stretch:** a full brand brief (colors, audience, tone) plus a two-sentence rationale
for every AI choice kept.
**They walk away with:** a portfolio-able artifact and the realization they can do this
for people they know.

### 3. Alt-text draft-and-revise `[assist→solo]` · 25 min

Accessibility is the humanization pillar wearing work clothes: AI drafts image
descriptions fast, and human judgment makes them true and useful.

**Run it:**
1. Play 30 seconds of a screen reader hitting an undescribed image — let the room feel
   the gap (5 min).
2. AI drafts alt text for four images; students correct each for accuracy, concision,
   and context — flagging what the AI missed or invented (15 min).
3. Compare corrections; the stretch tier tackles a chart, where context changes
   everything (5 min).

**Floor:** simple photos.
**Stretch:** charts and infographics; write alt text serving a specific document purpose.
**They walk away with:** a genuinely employable compliance skill and the pattern
*AI drafts, human owns*.
Sources: [Maricopa accessibility handbook](https://open.maricopa.edu/accessibilityhandbook/chapter/ai-generated-alt-text/), [JMU guide](https://www.jmu.edu/accessibility/digital-accessibility/fundamentals/guides/image-description/ai-generated-alt-text.shtml)

### 4. Fooled on purpose: Two Truths & AI → Detect Fakes `[show→solo]` · 25 min

Everyone gets fooled in a low-stakes room so they're harder to fool in a high-stakes
one.

**Run it:**
1. [Two Truths & AI](https://www.commonsense.org/games/two-truths-and-ai): three movie
   posters, one fake — class votes, then the tells are revealed (10 min).
2. Solo rounds of the [Kellogg Detect Fakes study](https://detectfakes.kellogg.northwestern.edu/?study=image)
   — students rate confidence and see how others voted; they're contributing to real
   research (10 min).
3. Debrief: the visual tells are dying as models improve — which is why provenance
   ([Content Credentials](https://verify.contentauthenticity.org/)) matters more than
   sharp eyes (5 min).

**Floor:** vote in the group round.
**Stretch:** articulate each specific tell, then explain why it won't survive next
year's models.
**They walk away with:** calibrated suspicion and one provenance tool.
