# Teaching scaffold & mixed-ability structures

Part of the [activity idea library](../ACTIVITY_CONCEPTS.md). Cross-cutting classroom
structures — session skeleton, task design, and group formats — for a class spanning
computer novices to engineers.


Reusable classroom structures for the wide skill spread (little computer experience →
engineering students), from adult-ed and tech-instruction teaching guides.

These aren't just teaching tricks — they mirror how real workplaces run technical work:
pair programming's driver/navigator roles, async peer review, and demo days all show up
below in classroom form.

**Scaling structures (if enrollment runs large)** *(instructor-seeded)*

- **A/B parallel rooms, one streamed lecture** — split the class across two rooms;
  one instructor delivers the lecture, streamed to the second room; each room has its
  own instructor for the lab block. Works because this is a lab-heavy course: the
  lecture is identical everywhere, and the entire value of a second instructor is
  circulation time during hands-on work — the student-to-helper ratio halves without
  doubling prep.
- **A/B/C interest tracks — art · engineering · science · business** — same module,
  same scaffold, track-flavored activities. The menus already support it: the flyer
  sprint and style studies serve an art track; local models, agent specs, and the n8n
  builds serve engineering; the climate-data labs serve science; email triage, the
  business briefs, and workflow mapping serve business. Tracks are themed
  choice boards (see Task design below) chosen by *interest, not skill level* — so
  there's no remedial stigma — and a student's track naturally feeds their capstone brief. Track
  tables or rooms can be led by TAs or industry-panel volunteers.

**Session skeleton**

- **Gradual release ("I do / we do / you do")** — model fully → do it together → practice
  alone, with an optional "you do it together" step between. Advanced students may skip
  ahead after demonstrating mastery in the "we do" step — same arc, no separate tracks.
  [LINCS/TEAL guide](https://lincs.ed.gov/state-resources/federal-initiatives/teal/guide/gradrelease)
- **BYOD pre-class buffer + tech-buddy triage** — 10–15 min before official start for
  device/account issues; a rotating "tech buddy" (confident student) handles individual
  troubleshooting so setup struggles don't eat instruction time — and the buddy role is a
  low-stakes leadership job. [CBT Nuggets BYOD](https://www.cbtnuggets.com/blog/technology/programming/creating-the-perfect-byod-environment)
- **Sandbox/open-lab back half** — After guided practice, an unstructured block where
  students work on their own material with instructor circulating. Novices iterate near
  the guided example; advanced students pivot to their real job task. [U-M MIDAS AI sandbox](https://midas.umich.edu/research/ai-sandbox/)
- **Real-time understanding checks every 15–20 min** — thumbs/mini-whiteboard polls
  between chunks; wave students who signal mastery into the extension task instead of
  re-explaining. [Bright Pathway FE differentiation](https://brightpathway.co.uk/differentiation-strategies-for-fe-and-adult-education/)
- **Exit tickets: muddiest point / one-minute paper / plus-delta (rotate them)** —
  anonymous confusion-flagging for novices, pacing feedback from advanced students,
  course-improvement data for the instructor. [NIU CATs](https://www.niu.edu/citl/resources/guides/examples-of-classroom-assessment-techniques.shtml), [Cornell MTEI](https://mtei.engineering.cornell.edu/tips-updates/muddiest-point-and-one-minute-papers-for-active-learning-and-feedback)
- **Parking lot** — visible space for deep/tangential questions ("how does the model
  actually work?") with a committed answer time; advanced curiosity gets served without
  derailing pace for novices. [Faculty Focus](https://www.facultyfocus.com/articles/effective-teaching-strategies/how-i-used-the-parking-lot-to-turn-quiet-rooms-into-engaged-classrooms/)

**Task design**

- **Low-floor / high-ceiling tasks** (Papert) — design the core lab so entry needs almost
  no prior skill but headroom is enormous; fast learners are never idle. [Math Learning Center](https://www.mathlearningcenter.org/blog/low-floorhigh-ceiling-tasks-other-takeaways)
- **Tiered task card on one shared sheet (core / extend / stretch)** — three depths of
  the same objective on the same page; novices stop after core and still hit the
  objective; nobody is visibly on a "remedial" worksheet. [Bright Pathway](https://brightpathway.co.uk/differentiation-strategies-for-fe-and-adult-education/)
- **Choice boards** — a grid where one center square is mandatory, the rest self-selected;
  differentiation without visible tracks. [OTAN adult-ed choice boards](https://otan.us/Resources/WebBasedClassActivity/Details/28?catId=0)
- **Scaffold levels that fade (describe → tier → assign → fade)** — same end task for
  everyone, different starter materials (worked example → template → checklist → blank),
  deliberately withdrawn over sessions. [Structural Learning](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/stretch-and-challenge-a-teachers-guide)
- **Andragogy framing: real task, not toy exercise** — every lab invites students to
  substitute their own document/problem; difficulty then scales automatically to what
  each student brings. [Knowles' andragogy](https://www.instructionaldesign.org/theories/andragogy/)
- **One-page reference card per tool** *(adapted)* — glance-not-read cheat sheet at every
  seat: working-memory safety net for novices, fast lookup for the advanced.

**Group structures**

- **Driver/navigator pairing** — two students, one device, swap mid-task; the navigator
  role (read, question, catch errors) gives the less-confident partner a real job that
  doesn't need typing speed. [Computing at School](https://www.computingatschool.org.uk/forum-news-blogs/2023/january/paired-programming-introducing-the-driver-navigator-roles/)
- **Think-pair-share / write-pair-share** — silent think protects slower processors and
  non-native speakers; the pair step lets advanced students tutor informally. [UGA active learning](https://activelearning.uga.edu/instructor-development/instructor-resources/think-pair-share)
- **Flexible needs-based grouping, re-formed every 2–3 sessions** — group by
  currently-observed gaps, not a fixed beginner/advanced label; nobody gets typecast.
  [Bright Pathway](https://brightpathway.co.uk/differentiation-strategies-for-fe-and-adult-education/)
- **Gallery walk with timed stations** — rotate through posted artifacts annotating;
  novices copy techniques by example, advanced students defend theirs. [Facing History](https://www.facinghistory.org/resource-library/gallery-walk-0)
- **Community-of-practice share-backs** — recurring 10–15 min segment where students
  present "here's what I tried" from between sessions; peer examples are less
  intimidating than polished expert demos. [UBC CTLT](https://ctlt.ubc.ca/programs/communities-of-practice/)
- **UDL triple representation** — say it, hand it out with a worked example, and show it
  live, every time; serves visual/auditory processors and mild sensory limits without
  singling anyone out. [CAST UDL](https://www.cast.org/what-we-do/artificial-intelligence/)
