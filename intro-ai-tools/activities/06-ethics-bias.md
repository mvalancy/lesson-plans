# Activity ideas: Module 6 — Ethics, Bias, and Responsible AI

Part of the [activity idea library](../ACTIVITY_CONCEPTS.md) — brainstorming
material, not finished lesson plans. Scaffold tags, floor/stretch notes, and the
three pillars (agency · ownership · humanization) are defined in the top-level map.

> **Deepening this file:** each module file is independent so a research agent can
> go deep on one lesson at a time. When adding ideas: keep the entry format, verify
> every link is live, mark inventions as *(adapted)*, and prefer activities with a
> low floor, a high ceiling, and a workplace or community tie-in.


- **Survival of the Best Fit** `[show or solo]` — Free 6-minute browser game: play a
  hiring manager who automates resume screening and watches it inherit your own biases.
  Stretch: open the GitHub repo and inspect the decision logic. Workplace: real ATS
  screening parallels. [survivalofthebestfit.com](https://www.survivalofthebestfit.com/)
  **Debrief structure comes free**: the game's own
  [resources page](https://www.survivalofthebestfit.com/resources) is written in three
  parts that map to a three-round class discussion — *Behind the Technology* (ML
  fundamentals, the black-box problem, historical data bias), *Fair Software* (why
  "technology alone" can't fix bias; accountability beyond engineering), and *Steps
  Forward* (public awareness, corporate accountability, diverse participation, education
  reform) — plus a full reading list on its GitHub wiki for the stretch tier.
- **Moral Machine structured lesson** `[assist groups]` — MIT's self-driving-car dilemma
  scenarios; vote as a team, compare your "ethics profile" to global data. Stretch:
  critique the trolley-problem framing itself. [teachwithict lesson](https://www.teachwithict.com/moral-machine.html), [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/moral-machine/overview/)
- **Image-generator bias audit** `[solo→assist]` *(adapted from published research)* —
  Identical neutral prompts ("a CEO," "a nurse") through a free image tool; tally apparent
  gender/skin tone; compare to real labor statistics. Stretch: critique your own sample
  size against the ["Stable Bias" paper's](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.11408) methodology.
  [Rest of World investigation](https://restofworld.org/2023/ai-image-stereotypes/)
- **Search-engine occupation screenshot audit** `[solo pairs]` *(adapted)* — Image-search
  "CEO"/"nurse"/"engineer," screenshot page one, compare representation to labor-force
  data; replicates the classic 2015 study. [UW iSchool follow-up](https://ischool.uw.edu/news/2022/02/googles-ceo-image-search-gender-bias-hasnt-really-been-fixed)
- **COMPAS fairness-metrics mini-lab** `[assist]` — Compute accuracy and false-positive/
  negative rates by race on the real recidivism dataset; discover "equal accuracy" hiding
  unequal error types. Dual-track: spreadsheet for non-coders, notebook for engineers.
  [Fairness book chapter](https://afraenkel.github.io/fairness-book/content/04-compas.html), [ProPublica methodology](https://www.propublica.org/article/how-we-analyzed-the-compas-recidivism-algorithm)
- **Resume-screening bias case study** `[assist]` — Published engineering-ethics lab:
  a ranking algorithm re-learns demographic bias even after names are removed. Floor:
  walkthrough of pre-run outputs. Stretch: modify the notebook. [ASEE case study](https://peer.asee.org/case-study-using-synthetic-datasets-to-examine-bias-in-machine-learning-algorithms-for-resume-screening)
- **Facial recognition role debate** `[assist]` — Campus-surveillance case with assigned
  stakeholder roles; advanced roles must cite the Gender Shades accuracy-disparity
  findings. [Online Ethics Center case](https://onlineethics.org/cases/george-mason-tech-ethics/utilizing-facial-recognition-university-campus), [Gender Shades](http://gendershades.org/docs/ibm.pdf)
- **Day of AI ethics debate** `[assist]` — Four stakeholder groups (tech companies,
  government, educators, consumers) argue real scenarios; compressible into one 95-min
  lab. Mirrors real cross-functional AI-adoption debates inside companies. [Day of AI unit](https://dayofai.org/units/ai-ethics-debate)
- **"Algorithms as opinions": redesign a recommender** `[assist groups]` — Map YouTube's
  stakeholders, then redesign its recommendation algorithm to serve them — algorithms as
  opinions, not neutral math. [MIT Media Lab AI ethics curriculum](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/ai-ethics-for-middle-school/overview/)
- **Hopes & Concerns mural** `[show]` — Sticky-note warm-up clustering the class's hopes
  and worries about AI; the change-management "concerns audit" companies run before
  rollouts. Pairs directly with the Winthrop agency-first framing. [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/ai-ethics-for-middle-school/overview/)
- **AI art & artists' rights debate** `[assist]` *(adapted)* — Four-corners debate:
  uncompensated training data vs. fair use vs. opt-out/compensation schemes. Workplace:
  licensing exposure of AI-generated marketing assets. [Georgetown JIA](https://gjia.georgetown.edu/2024/07/10/innovation-and-artists-rights-in-the-age-of-generative-ai/), [Yale Law Journal forum](https://yalelawjournal.org/forum/artificial-why-copyright-is-not-the-right-policy-tool-to-deal-with-generative-ai)
- **Data Nutrition Label critique** `[assist]` — Apply the real "nutrition facts for
  datasets" framework to a dataset; stretch: audit an actual public dataset's
  documentation gaps. "What was this trained on, and what are its blind spots?"
  [datanutrition.org](https://datanutrition.org/)
- **AI Incident Database investigation** `[solo→assist]` — Pick a real documented AI
  failure (2,000+ reports); present what went wrong, who was harmed, what would have
  prevented it. A published study ran this as an 80-minute module. [incidentdatabase.ai](https://incidentdatabase.ai/), [study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.06269)
- **Data Detox Kit stations** `[solo stations]` — Tactical Tech's free printable
  privacy/security/wellbeing workshop, modular by design. Workplace: data hygiene and
  what to share with AI tools. [datadetoxkit.org](https://datadetoxkit.org/)
- **Clickwrap challenge: read the actual ToS** `[solo→assist]` *(adapted)* — 5 minutes
  with a real AI tool's terms: what's collected, who owns input/output, does it train
  future models? Floor: highlighted excerpt. Stretch: full document unaided. Workplace:
  exactly what employees pasting company data need to know.
- **Draft your own AI use policy** `[solo + peer review]` *(adapted)* — One-page personal
  or team policy: acceptable uses, data rules, disclosure norms, escalation. A literal
  workplace deliverable and a strong module closer. Floor: fill a template. Stretch:
  blank page, justify each rule. [AIHR template](https://www.aihr.com/blog/ai-policy-template/), [syllabus-policy models](https://celt.uky.edu/ai-course-policy-examples)
- **Energy & water footprint calculator** `[assist]` — Estimate the footprint of your own
  week of AI queries; scale to class- or company-wide; stretch: challenge the estimates'
  methodology. The topic most comparable courses omit. [UNU explainer](https://unu.edu/inweh/news/environmental-cost-of-AIs-Enrgy-use-carbon-water-and-land-footprints), [lesson plan base](https://ailiteracyshop.com/blog/ai-environmental-impact-lesson-middle-school/)
- **Four Corners AI ethics protocol** `[show]` — "Companies should be required to label
  all AI content" → walk to Strongly Agree/Agree/Disagree/Strongly Disagree and defend.
  Tier the statements: workplace scenarios for novices, policy/technical for advanced.
  [Four corners method](https://ggie.berkeley.edu/practice/four-corners/)
- **Academic-integrity disclosure ladder** `[assist card-sort]` *(adapted from research)* —
  Rank scenarios from spellcheck → AI outline → AI-drafted section → fully AI-generated;
  does disclosure change the ranking? (Research finds perceived ethicality rises sharply
  with verification + acknowledgment.) Parallels workplace disclosure norms. [Frontiers study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1621743/full)

**The games shelf** — playable browser games in the Survival of the Best Fit family, each
a self-contained `[show]` warm-up or `[solo]` station with a debrief. All links verified
live. (Also on this shelf but listed in other modules: Two Truths & AI, MIT Detect Fakes,
Human or Not, Quick Draw!, Teachable Machine. Building our own game in this genre — they
are just single-page JS — would itself make a strong future course artifact.)

- [Survival of the Best Fit](https://www.survivalofthebestfit.com/game/) — hiring
  automation inherits your bias; people become numbers. The humanization anti-pattern.
- [How Normal Am I](https://hownormalami.eu/) — an EU-funded face-judging demo that
  scores your age/beauty/BMI live on camera, then reveals how such judgments are used.
- [Gandalf (Lakera)](https://gandalf.lakera.ai/) — trick an AI guarding a password:
  prompt injection as a game; why "the AI will enforce the rules" is not a safety plan.
- [Which Face Is Real?](https://whichfaceisreal.com/) — GAN face vs. real photo, from
  the UW "Calling Bullshit" course team.
- [Spot the Troll](https://spotthetroll.org/) — real vs. troll social accounts (Clemson
  Media Forensics Hub).
- [Bad News](https://www.getbadnews.com/) — play a misinformation-monger; inoculation
  by building the thing (Cambridge research-backed).
- [Harmony Square](https://harmonysquare.game/) — disinformation officer sows discord
  in an idyllic town; from the same research group.
- [Fake It To Make It](https://www.fakeittomakeitgame.com/) — run a fake-news site for
  profit; the economics of misinformation.
- [Moderator Mayhem](https://moderatormayhem.engine.is/) — content-moderation decisions
  under time pressure; why "just moderate better" is hard.
- [Trust & Safety Tycoon](https://trustandsafety.fun/) — run a platform's trust &
  safety team; policy tradeoffs made playable.
- [The Evolution of Trust](https://ncase.me/trust/) — Nicky Case's game-theory
  masterpiece on how trust builds and breaks; frames why systems shape behavior.
- [Parable of the Polygons](https://ncase.me/polygons/) — tiny individual biases produce
  segregated systems; the emergent-harm intuition behind algorithmic bias.
- [Semantle](https://semantle.com/) — guess the secret word by embedding similarity;
  sneaks the concept of embeddings into a party game (also fits Module 1).
- [Universal Paperclips](https://www.decisionproblem.com/paperclips/index2.html) — the
  runaway-optimizer thought experiment as an addictive incremental game.
- [Interland (Be Internet Awesome)](https://beinternetawesome.withgoogle.com/en_us/interland) —
  Google's digital-citizenship game; aimed younger but a gentle floor for true novices.
