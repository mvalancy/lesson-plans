# Introduction to AI Tools

A hands-on, plain-language course on the AI tools reshaping business, education, health, science, and the arts. How to own them rather than merely subscribe to them, and how to point them at the drudgery so your attention goes back to people. No coding background required.

[Start Module 1](https://lessons.mattvalancy.com/intro-ai-tools/lessons/01-what-is-ai.html) [Watch the 15-min mini-lesson](https://lessons.mattvalancy.com/intro-ai-tools/mini-lesson/)

8 modules ~3 hours each No coding required Free tools and open models

## What this course is

A survey of what AI actually is and how it fits into ordinary work. Each module pairs a short, jargon-free lecture with a much longer lab where you use the tools yourself (writing, research, data analysis, creative work) while learning to check what comes back for accuracy, bias, and ethical problems.

By the end of the course you will be able to:

-   **Use** AI tools to complete real tasks in writing, research, data analysis, and creative expression.
-   **Evaluate** AI outputs for accuracy, bias, and ethical implications, and justify your judgment.
-   **Own** what you build with them: understand the parts, know your alternatives, and keep the result.
-   **Decide** when _not_ to use them at all, and spend the time you save on people rather than on more output.

## How this course thinks

Most AI teaching stops at operating the software. Three commitments run underneath every module here, and they are the reason the labs are shaped the way they are.

-   01
    
    ### Agency
    
    The first question is never _how_ to use an AI tool. It is _whether_, and for what. You will practise saying no to these tools as deliberately as you practise using them, because a choice you cannot decline is not a choice.
    
-   02
    
    ### Ownership
    
    The chip in a current tablet or laptop will already run a capable model without touching the internet, and that capability gets cheaper every year. Learn the parts (models, gateways, open ecosystems), run one yourself, and publish what you make somewhere you control.
    
-   03
    
    ### Humanization
    
    Use the machine on the drudgery, so your attention goes to people. Send the one-page answer somebody actually wanted, not the thousand words a model will happily generate. And never let a system quietly turn a person into a number.
    

Within a few years, serious AI will be an ordinary household appliance. The people who understand what it is made of, and what it is _for_, will be the ones deciding how it gets used at home, at work, and in their communities. None of that requires code, a budget, or a computer science degree.

## The eight modules

Each module page carries its objectives, a minute-by-minute agenda for a three-hour session, lab instructions, and homework. Enough for someone else to pick it up and teach it.

1.  Module 1
    
    ### [What is AI? Past, Present, and Future](https://lessons.mattvalancy.com/intro-ai-tools/lessons/01-what-is-ai.html)
    
    Definitions, machine vs. human intelligence, and the key terms: LLMs, machine learning, neural nets.
    
2.  Module 2
    
    ### [Generative AI for Everyday Productivity](https://lessons.mattvalancy.com/intro-ai-tools/lessons/02-everyday-productivity.html)
    
    Text generation, summarization, and translation, including the discipline of sending less, not more.
    
3.  Module 3
    
    ### [Working with AI Images, Audio, and Video](https://lessons.mattvalancy.com/intro-ai-tools/lessons/03-images-audio-video.html)
    
    Diffusion models, speech-to-text, text-to-speech, and how to spot a deepfake.
    
4.  Module 4
    
    ### [AI and Data: Spreadsheets & Visualization](https://lessons.mattvalancy.com/intro-ai-tools/lessons/04-spreadsheets-data.html)
    
    Using AI in Excel and Google Sheets, basic data literacy, and chart generation.
    
5.  Mini-lesson Module 5
    
    ### [AI for Research and Information Gathering](https://lessons.mattvalancy.com/intro-ai-tools/lessons/05-research-information.html)
    
    How LLMs search, why they hallucinate, and how to fact-check everything they tell you.
    
6.  Module 6
    
    ### [Ethics, Bias, and Responsible AI](https://lessons.mattvalancy.com/intro-ai-tools/lessons/06-ethics-bias.html)
    
    Bias, privacy, copyright, and environmental cost, plus the systems that quietly turn people into numbers.
    
7.  Module 7
    
    ### [Automation with AI Assistants](https://lessons.mattvalancy.com/intro-ai-tools/lessons/07-automation.html)
    
    Workflow automation, Zapier, and AI copilots. Hand the drudgery to the machine and get your attention back.
    
8.  Module 8
    
    ### [Capstone Showcase](https://lessons.mattvalancy.com/intro-ai-tools/lessons/08-capstone.html)
    
    Design, build, and present your own AI-assisted project: a study aid, creative piece, or workflow.

## How to use this course

Everything here is free, and it is meant to be taken. Work through the modules on your own, or lift them wholesale and run the course for a club, a library, a classroom, or a group of friends. Attribution is welcome, a permission slip is not required.

-   **Learning alone?** Follow the module pages in order and actually do the labs. The reading is short; the doing is the course.
-   **Teaching it?** Each module page is a lesson plan, with a timed agenda you can teach straight from. Start with the [mini-lesson](https://lessons.mattvalancy.com/intro-ai-tools/mini-lesson/) to see the format.
-   **Materials.** No textbook purchase. The recommended companion is [Elements of AI](https://www.elementsofai.com/) (University of Helsinki and MinnaLearn; no affiliation, it just teaches well). Beginner-friendly, no math. Everything else is on the [Resources](https://lessons.mattvalancy.com/resources.html) page.
